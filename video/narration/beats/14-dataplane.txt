Stage thirteen. On every node, something watches that EndpointSlice and programs the local data plane so traffic to the Service's virtual address reaches a real pod IP. Classically that is kube-proxy, writing iptables or IPVS or, in current clusters, nftables rules. An eBPF data plane does the same job differently — translating the address at the socket layer, before packets are built, and keeping connection state in its own maps rather than netfilter's. eBPF does not remove connection tracking; it moves where the state lives. And the Service contract does not change when the backend does. These are implementations, not APIs.
